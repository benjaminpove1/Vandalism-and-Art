# Vandalism-and-Art

🌍 Turning vandalism into protected urban art through photocatalytic paint and community action in Ecuador.

## 📌 About the Project
Vandalism-and-Art is a project focused on recovering public spaces in Quito —especially the colonial historic center— by replacing vandalism with authorized murals created by qualified artists.

We use photocatalytic paint that absorbs pollution and stays clean, while building institutional support, community ownership, and clear protection mechanisms so the murals last.

**Mission:** Transform damaged walls into protected cultural assets that improve the city instead of degrading it.

## ⚠️ The Problem
Vandalism and pollution affect public spaces across Ecuador, particularly in Quito’s historic center (a UNESCO World Heritage site).

This creates:
- Deteriorated walls, facades, and public areas
- High and repeated cleaning costs
- Loss of cultural and touristic value
- Reduced sense of safety and belonging
- Missed opportunities for positive artistic expression

## 💡 Our Approach
1. **Qualified artists** — Open call for muralists, urban artists, and art students with portfolios and experience.
2. **Institutional agreement (Convenio)** — Partnership with the Ministry of Culture and Heritage, the Metropolitan Institute of Heritage (IMP), and/or the Municipality of Quito.
3. **Authorized spaces** — Official assignment of facades, walls, and public surfaces for murals.
4. **Photocatalytic paint** — Self-cleaning paint that absorbs pollutants and makes graffiti harder to stick and easier to remove.
5. **Protection & signage** — Official plaques, community involvement, and institutional backing to reduce vandalism.
6. **Promotion** — Before/after documentation and public communication about the paint and the murals.

## 🎯 Goals
- Reduce vandalism by offering a positive and authorized alternative
- Recover and enhance the colonial historic center of Quito
- Decrease surface and nearby air pollution
- Give qualified artists real spaces and materials to work
- Create lasting, protected murals with community ownership
- Promote photocatalytic paint as a practical urban tool

## 🏙️ Zone Selection Criteria
Areas are chosen based on:
- Current level of vandalism
- Cultural or touristic value (priority: Quito Historic Center)
- Feasibility of official authorization
- Accessibility and safety for artists
- Community interest and support

## 🎨 Mural Themes
Suggested themes aligned with Quito’s identity and recovery:
- Cultural heritage and colonial architecture of Quito
- Andean nature and Ecuadorian biodiversity
- Mestizo identity and cultural diversity
- Care for public space and the environment
- Historical figures and moments of Ecuador

## 🛡️ How We Reduce Vandalism
While no project can guarantee 100% protection, we significantly lower the risk through:
- Official authorization and institutional convenio
- Visible signage marking the mural as protected
- Photocatalytic paint that resists and simplifies graffiti removal
- Community participation and ownership
- Initial monitoring with municipal or collective support

## 🇨🇱 Inspiration
The project draws inspiration from Chile’s strong muralism tradition (including the Brigada Ramona Parra and contemporary urban art), where murals have been used as tools for identity, recovery, and positive cultural expression.

## 🌱 Expected Impact
By combining authorized art, photocatalytic paint, and institutional support, Vandalism-and-Art aims to:
- Improve the visual and environmental quality of public spaces
- Strengthen cultural identity in Quito’s historic center
- Offer artists real opportunities
- Reduce long-term cleaning costs
- Shift the perception of walls from targets of vandalism to canvases for positive art
